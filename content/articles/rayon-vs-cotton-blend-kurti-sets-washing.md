Indian summers are hard on daily-wear kurtis. Water is often hard, machines are small top-loaders with aggressive spin cycles, and clothes dry on balconies in direct sun. Under those conditions the fabric your set is made of matters more than the print on it, and the two fabrics that dominate budget listings behave very differently once the wash bucket comes out.

## How Each Fabric Behaves in Water

Rayon is a regenerated cellulose fibre, and its weak point is exactly the one Indian washing exposes: it loses much of its strength while wet. A rayon kurta that feels smooth and substantial when you first open the packet becomes noticeably fragile in the wash bucket. Pulling a heavy wet rayon kurta out of the machine and wringing it by hand is where most of the damage happens, not in the drum itself.

Cotton blends, usually cotton mixed with polyester or viscose in varying ratios, react the opposite way. The synthetic content gives the weave a memory, so the garment holds its shape through a spin cycle instead of stretching out of it. The trade-off is feel: a cotton blend is crisper and slightly stiffer against the skin on day one, and it drapes less flatteringly than rayon.

## The Ten-Wash Test

Here is what actually separates the two after a few weeks of regular wear:

- **Colour and print.** Rayon takes dye beautifully, so a printed rayon set looks rich out of the packet. But frequent hot washes fade rayon prints faster, especially fine block-style motifs. Cotton blend prints dull more slowly because the print sits on a more stable surface.
- **Shape.** A rayon palazzo washed hot and hung in full sun slowly loses its taper and can shrink at the hem. A cotton blend palazzo keeps its cut far longer; the blend resists both shrinkage and stretch.
- **Creases.** This is rayon's daily annoyance. Every rayon kurta comes out of the wash crumpled, and ironing becomes part of the routine. A cotton blend needs less ironing to look presentable, which is why college students who wash twice a week tend to drift toward blends.
- **Dupattas.** A cotton dupatta survives nearly any wash you throw at it. A thin rayon or viscose dupatta is usually the first casualty in a set, tearing near the tassels within a season.

## Care Rules That Decide How Long a Rayon Set Lasts

If you buy rayon, the washing method matters more than the fabric itself.

- **Wash cold, gently.** Cold water and a short, gentle cycle (or a hand swish in a bucket) keep wet rayon from over-stressing. Hot water is the fastest way to shrink it.
- **Never wring.** Roll the wet kurta in a dry towel and press, or hang it dripping. Twisting wet rayon is what breaks down the fibre at the seams.
- **Dry in shade.** Direct balcony sun fades prints and stiffens rayon. Dry it inside out, in the shade, on a hanger that matches the shoulder width.
- **Iron while slightly damp.** Rayon irons out beautifully with a little moisture left in it and stubbornly when fully dry.

A cotton blend forgives almost all of these mistakes. You can machine wash it hot, spin it hard, and dry it in the sun and the worst outcome is a slightly duller print over a season.

## Which One to Buy for Which Use

The honest answer is that this is not a which-is-better comparison; it is a which-do-you-wash comparison.

Buy rayon when the set is for occasions, photos, or wear where drape carries the look, and you are willing to hand-wash it or run a gentle cycle. A well-kept rayon set looks noticeably more premium per rupee than a blend.

Buy a cotton blend when the set is your Monday-to-Friday uniform: daily college or office wear, frequent washing, no ironing patience. It will look plainer in the listing photos and noticeably better on week six.

A practical middle path is the mixed set: rayon kurta, cotton dupatta. You keep the drape where it shows and hand the most wash-abused piece to the fabric that survives it.
